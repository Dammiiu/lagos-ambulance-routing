CHAPTER ONE 
INTRODUCTION 
1.1 Background to the Study 
Emergency medical services (EMS) play a critical role in ensuring timely medical 
intervention, where even slight delays in response time can significantly affect patient 
survival outcomes. In urban environments characterized by rapid population growth, 
traffic congestion, and complex road networks, the efficiency of ambulance routing has 
become a major concern. Traditional dispatch systems often rely on simple distance-
based routing or human judgment, which fails to account for dynamic road conditions, 
network constraints, and travel time variability. As a result, there is an increasing need 
for intelligent systems that can optimize ambulance routing using advanced spatial and 
computational techniques. 
Recent advancements in Geographic Information Systems (GIS) and network analysis 
have provided powerful tools for modeling transportation networks and improving 
emergency response efficiency. Studies such as Mappouras et al. (2025) demonstrate 
the integration of GIS with Artificial Intelligence to create decision-support systems 
capable of optimizing ambulance dispatch and routing using real-time data. Similarly, 
Alamri (2023) developed a spatial routing system that combines Voronoi-based 
accessibility modeling with time-dependent Dijkstra algorithms to improve emergency 
service delivery. These approaches emphasize that response time, rather than distance, 
is the most critical factor in emergency routing. 
Further research has highlighted the importance of network-based analysis in 
understanding accessibility and optimizing routes. Paul-Silviu NICOARĂ (2014) 
demonstrated how GIS-based shortest path algorithms can effectively determine

infrastructure limitations. Other studies, such as Hao et al. (2024), have shown that

including Ant Colony Optimization and machine learning models, have been explored 
to improve routing efficiency under dynamic conditions (Luo et al., 2026; Métwalli et 
al., 2026). 
Beyond routing, spatial analysis techniques have also been applied to understand the

government settings. Moreover, there remains a gap in developing simplified, GIS-

coverage analysis without requiring extensive real-time data integration.

1.10 Definition of Terms 
1. GIS (Geographic Information System): A system for capturing, storing, 
analyzing, and visualizing spatial data.  
2. Network Analysis: A GIS technique used to model and analyze movement 
through a network such as roads.  
3. Impedance: The cost associated with traveling through a network, typically 
measured in time or distance.  
4. Service Area: A region reachable within a specified travel time from a facility.  
5. Closest Facility Analysis: A method used to identify the nearest service 
location based on network travel cost.  
6. Emergency Response Time: The time taken for an ambulance to reach an 
incident location after dispatch.

2.2 Geographic Information Systems (GIS) in Emergency Services 
Geographic Information Systems (GIS) have emerged as a critical tool in the 
management and optimization of emergency services due to their ability to integrate 
spatial and non-spatial data for decision-making. Several studies have demonstrated the 
effectiveness of GIS in modeling emergency response systems and improving 
operational efficiency. 
Mappouras et al. (2025) developed a GIS-integrated decision support system that 
leverages spatial data and artificial intelligence to optimize ambulance dispatch. Their 
study highlights how GIS enables real-time visualization and improved coordination in 
emergency situations. Similarly, Popescu & Bărbulescu (2025)utilized GIS-based 
spatial analysis to determine optimal routes to emergency healthcare facilities, 
emphasizing the importance of spatial accuracy in route planning. 
In addition, Pérez-Fernández et al. (2025)applied GIS techniques for spatio-temporal 
analysis of emergency incidents, revealing patterns that can inform strategic planning.

constrained environments. This highlights the need for simplified GIS-based solutions 
that can still provide effective routing and analysis. 
2.3 Network Analysis in Transportation Systems 
Network analysis is a fundamental component of GIS used to model transportation 
systems and optimize routing decisions. It enables the representation of road networks 
as interconnected nodes and edges, facilitating the computation of optimal paths based 
on defined impedance factors such as distance or time. 
Alamri (2023) applied a time-dependent Dijkstra algorithm within a GIS framework to 
optimize emergency vehicle routing. The study demonstrated that travel-time-based 
routing significantly outperforms distance-based approaches in real-world scenarios. 
Similarly, Amoakoh et al. (2024) emphasized the role of road network topology and 
traffic conditions in influencing route efficiency. 
Advanced optimization techniques such as Ant Colony Optimization have also been 
explored for routing problems. Métwalli et al. (2026) showed that bio-inspired 
algorithms can dynamically adapt to changing conditions, improving route selection. 
However, these methods often require high computational resources and complex 
implementation. 
Overall, while network analysis provides a strong foundation for routing optimization, 
there remains a need for practical implementations that balance efficiency and 
simplicity.

2.4 Ambulance Routing Optimization Techniques 
Ambulance routing optimization has been widely studied using various computational 
and analytical techniques. Traditional approaches often rely on shortest path algorithms 
such as Dijkstra and A*, while modern approaches incorporate machine learning and 
heuristic optimization methods. 
Luo et al. (2026) explored machine learning-based routing systems that predict optimal 
routes based on historical traffic data. Similarly, Muhammed & Okunola (2020) applied 
predictive analytics to improve emergency response strategies. These approaches 
demonstrate the potential of intelligent systems in enhancing routing efficiency. 
However, many of these methods depend on large datasets and real-time data 
integration, which may not always be available. In contrast, GIS-based network 
analysis provides a more accessible alternative that can still achieve significant 
improvements in routing performance. 
2.5 Emergency Response Time Analysis 
Emergency response time is a critical performance metric in healthcare systems, 
directly impacting patient survival rates. Several studies have focused on analyzing and 
reducing response times using spatial and computational methods. 
Ragsdale & Russell (2022) developed an accessibility-based model to evaluate 
emergency response coverage, highlighting the importance of travel time as a key 
determinant. Prasetyo Wibowo Yunanto (2025) introduced stochastic modeling 
techniques to predict response times under uncertainty. 
These studies emphasize that response time analysis must account for multiple factors, 
including road conditions, traffic patterns, and spatial distribution of facilities. 
However, many models are complex and difficult to implement in practice, reinforcing 
the need for simpler, GIS-based approaches.

2.6 Spatial Accessibility and Service Area Analysis 
Spatial accessibility analysis evaluates the ease with which emergency services can 
reach different locations within a given area. Service area modeling is commonly used 
to determine coverage zones based on travel time thresholds. 
Alamri (2023) utilized Voronoi diagrams to model accessibility regions, while Urfalı 
& Eymen (2025) applied advanced accessibility metrics to assess healthcare coverage. 
These approaches provide valuable insights into service distribution and help identify 
underserved areas. 
Service area analysis within GIS allows for the visualization of coverage zones, 
typically represented as travel-time buffers (e.g., 5, 10, 15 minutes). This method is 
particularly useful for planning and resource allocation.
